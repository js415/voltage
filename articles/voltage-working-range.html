Understanding Working Range: 80V-300V vs 130V-300V

The working range tells you the voltage band in which a stabilizer can safely operate. Choosing the right range depends on how unstable the electricity supply is in your area.

What 80V-300V means

An 80V-300V stabilizer is a wide-range model. It is useful in areas where voltage drops very low, especially during summer evenings, heavy local load, or weak supply conditions.

What 130V-300V means

A 130V-300V stabilizer is suitable for areas where voltage does fluctuate but usually does not fall below 130V. It is a practical option for many homes with moderate voltage variation.

Which one should you choose?

If your lights dim often or your AC struggles to start, choose a wide-range model. If your voltage remains reasonably steady, a standard range model may be enough.

Ask before buying

Share your appliance type, tonnage, and local voltage condition with the dealer. The correct stabilizer protects better and avoids overspending on the wrong model.
